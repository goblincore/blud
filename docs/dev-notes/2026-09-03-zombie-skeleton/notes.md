# Zombie skeleton re-author — notes

**Date:** 2026-09-03 · **Branch:** off `claude/bone-tubes` · **Owner brief:** a standard human torso skeleton — twelve rib pairs wrapping spine → flank → sternum, upper ribs short and flat, middle widest, lower shorter and angled down; clavicles; a pelvis with two wide iliac wings over a sacrum, joining low at the pubis; the cage about as wide as the torso.

## What was wrong

Bone tubes (2026-09-02) made the field's skeleton visible for the first time, and the owner read the ribcage as "way off". Measured from the rest pose: six rib pairs over y 1.12–1.32 (20 cm of a 34 cm spine), ~12 cm across inside a 38 cm torso, each rib ONE quadratic from spine to sternum (7 cm chord, 8 cm bulge) so it read as a rod radiating from the spine. No clavicles. Pelvis = two 7 cm blades and a basin.

## What changed

- **`src/lab/sdf-zombie/characters/zombie.blob`** — the `bones` block's torso section is re-authored (skull and gut coil untouched). Everything below the `generated by scripts/zombie-skeleton-gen.ts` marker down to `GUT COIL` is emitted; the prose header above it says why.
- **`scripts/zombie-skeleton-gen.ts`** (new) — the anatomical parametrisation. A per-rib table (head height, half-width, half-depth, descent, radius, sternal/costal end) plus the spine curve, sternum, clavicles and pelvis landmarks; it emits the `.blob` lines and checks every prim with `boneBreach` (the sampler `checkBoneContainment` uses) against the full flesh AND a torso-only field (so a rib cannot hide inside the arm flesh that merges with the chest above y 1.10). `--check` splices into `zombie.blob` in memory and runs the real build; `--check --write` saves it.
- **`src/lab/sdf-zombie/rig-bind.ts`** — torso/head bones now bind rigidly to the nearest **axial** joint (a joint of an unmirrored bone: pelvis, spine, neck, skull), never a hip or shoulder. A hoop half's chord midpoint sits at the flank, nearer the hip/shoulder than any spine joint, and hips/shoulders take their own gait offsets — so with the old nearest-any-joint rule the widened ribs would have sheared exactly as the per-end bind did. Pinned in `rig-bind-bones.test.ts`.
- **`src/lab/sdf-zombie/webgpu/game-main.ts`** — bone instancer cap 512 → 1024. 82 drawn bones per body × 10 = 820 (was 380).
- **`src/lab/sdf-zombie/characters/zombie-blob.test.ts`** — pins the structure: ≥ 44 rib prims, ≥ 20 cm of cage, flank past x 0.15 both sides, every true-rib back half meets a front half at the flank within 1 mm, clavicles reaching the shoulder, a pelvis with ≥ 12 prims flaring past x 0.12 and reaching below y 0.94, flesh + bone ≤ 120.
- **`scripts/sdf-game-skeleton-capture.mjs`** (new) — the capture driver: frozen game page, tubes on, skin frames then skeleton frames (all flesh hidden), front / side / back / two close-ups.

## Round 2 — owner's first look at the twelve-rib cage (2026-09-03, in-tab)

Owner: "the ribcage has a top and bottom half and they shear — the rig allows movements that should not happen"; "too many ribs, maybe half, thicker"; "the pelvis needs to be a big solid mass, not doodly lines"; "shading is still uniform — should have random red bits like the field bones".

1. **Shear.** Root cause was the bind, not the rig: a bone prim bound to a rig POINT carries no rotation, so when the spine tilted the ribs bound to the chest point and the ribs bound to the hips point translated with their points and slid past each other. `rig-bind.ts` now gives every torso/head bone a `BoneFrame`: the axial segment (pelvis / spine / neck) nearest its midpoint, and rest endpoints relative to that segment's head. `applyRig` poses it as one rotation (body yaw first, then the shortest-arc residual from the turned rest direction to the live segment direction — the same composition as the rigid head) plus the segment head's translation, and stamps the rotation as `orient` so scaled bones turn with it. `rig-bind-bones.test.ts` tilts the chest point 20° about the hips and checks every rib-to-rib distance on the spine segment is unchanged.
2. **Six ribs, thicker.** Heads y 1.375 → 1.165 at 4.2 cm pitch, r 0.0095–0.011 × tall 1.3 (~28 mm band). Ribs 1–4 to the sternum, 5–6 to the costal margin. The fatter band clipped the flesh's back-flank corner (probed: at y 1.19 the half-width is 0.181 at z 0 but 0.139 at z −0.07), so the back half of every rib is a flattened arc (`BACK_FLAT` 0.78) rather than a true ellipse — nearer a real kidney-section thorax anyway.
3. **Solid pelvis.** Fat sacrum wedge (r 0.032, 48 mm across), per side an iliac blade (r 0.030 bar hub → crest apex, flattened front-to-back) with a thick crest rim and a bar back to the sacro-iliac joint, and a fat pubic ring (r 0.015–0.022). 68 bone prims total now (23 + 68 = 91 of 128); 600 tubes on the cast.
4. **Mottle.** `bone-instancer.ts` shade: two octaves of value noise on world position — a broad stain wash plus tight blood flecks where the noise peaks, weaker under a crater's centre. The helpers are their own WGSL strings (`BONE_HASH_WGSL`, `BONE_NOISE_WGSL`) chained as includes: `wgslFn` takes ONE fn per string and read a second fn's params as inputs, and the first boot drew nothing (pipeline failed, no exception — only a console error). `bone-instancer.test.ts` now pins one `fn` per string.
5. The generator's containment check is now 1.2 mm stricter than the build's: the emitted numbers round to 4 decimals and the compiled prim lands ~0.2 mm off, and rib 5 once passed the generator at 4.0 mm and failed the build at 3.93 mm.

Captures 01–07 were re-taken with all four changes; the console is clean with tubes on.

## Verdict (owner, 2026-09-03, after round 2)

"An improvement for sure but still not there yet... good enough for now to merge into main. The tubes aren't good enough to replace the current system yet." Two specific limits of the tube renderer, recorded for whoever picks this up:

- **Tubes cannot make a solid mass.** A pelvis built from capsules is "a messy line drawing"; the field's smooth-min could fuse those masses, the instancer cannot. The fix is not more/fatter tubes — it is a second instanced primitive (a plate / ellipsoid mesh, or an SDF-fused mesh baked per body) for the sacrum + ilia.
- **The cage still reads as spiky tubes that go in and out of sync.** The halves are rigid with the spine segment now (pinned), so what remains is the flesh moving against a rigid cage, and each hoop being two capsules that meet at a point rather than one continuous band. A single closed-loop tube per rib (a cubic or a polyline sweep) would remove the join.

Ships with `setBoneMesh` default OFF, as before. Merged to main 2026-09-03.
